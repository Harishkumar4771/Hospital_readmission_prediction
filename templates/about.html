About the Project

Predicting hospital readmission with machine learning.

Hospital readmissions within 30 days are one of the most expensive and preventable problems in healthcare. In the United States alone, they cost over $26 billion annually. Early identification of high-risk patients allows clinical teams to intervene before discharge — arranging follow-ups, adjusting medications, and coordinating post-discharge care.

MedPredict is a final-year PBL-1 project developed at the Symbiosis Institute of Technology, Pune, by four students from the Department of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning (Batch 2024–28). We built an end-to-end ML pipeline — from raw data to a deployed web application — that predicts whether a patient will be readmitted within 30 days of discharge.

Problem Statement

Why does this problem matter?

Hospital readmission within 30 days is a globally recognised quality indicator. Reducing it improves patient outcomes, reduces costs, and signals better continuity of care.

The Challenge

Clinicians must discharge hundreds of patients daily without a reliable, systematic way to identify which ones are most likely to return within 30 days. This decision is currently made largely on intuition and experience — leading to missed high-risk cases and avoidable readmissions that burden both the patient and the healthcare system.

Our Solution

A machine learning model trained on 25,000 hospital admission records that analyses a patient's clinical profile — including diagnosis, medications, lab results, and prior visit history — to output a readmission probability score. Paired with an AI-generated clinical summary, it gives clinicians an actionable risk report in under 10 seconds.

Why Recall Over Accuracy?

In healthcare, the cost of a false negative — predicting a high-risk patient as safe — is far greater than a false positive. A missed high-risk patient may deteriorate and return in a worse condition. We therefore tuned our model to maximise recall on the readmitted class, accepting lower specificity as a deliberate clinical trade-off.

Threshold Tuning

Rather than using the conventional 0.5 classification threshold, we performed a systematic sweep from 0.30 to 0.70 and identified 0.42 as the optimal threshold — achieving 75% recall on the readmitted class while maintaining ~60% overall accuracy, a meaningful 7-point improvement over the 53% majority-class baseline.

Limitations

What this model cannot do.

Understanding a model's limitations is as important as understanding its capabilities. We are transparent about these constraints.

No Social Context

The dataset contains no information about socioeconomic status, housing, social support, or lifestyle — all of which are significant readmission predictors in real clinical research.

Single Dataset

Trained on one publicly available dataset. Performance may differ on data from other hospitals, regions, or healthcare systems with different demographic compositions.

60% Accuracy

Overall accuracy of 60% is modest. Hospital readmission is a complex clinical event influenced by factors beyond any single admission record. Perfect prediction is not realistically achievable.

Not a Clinical Tool

MedPredict is a student research project. It has not undergone clinical validation, regulatory review, or safety testing required for use in actual patient care settings.

Low Specificity

The 0.42 threshold increases recall at the cost of specificity (47%). This means about half of non-readmitted patients are incorrectly flagged — a meaningful false alarm rate.

Static Model

The model is trained once on a fixed snapshot of data. It does not update with new patient outcomes. In a real deployment, periodic retraining would be essential.
