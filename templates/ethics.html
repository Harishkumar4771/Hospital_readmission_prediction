Responsible AI

Ethics, limitations &
responsible use.

We believe transparency is as important as performance. This page explains what MedPredict can and cannot do, how it should and should not be used, and the ethical principles that guided its design.

Important Disclaimer

MedPredict is a student research project built for the PBL-1 programme at Symbiosis Institute of Technology, Pune. It is trained on a single publicly available dataset and has not been validated in any clinical setting. It must not be used to make real medical decisions about real patients. Always consult a qualified healthcare professional.

Appropriate vs. Inappropriate Use

This tool is for

  • Academic research and learning about ML in healthcare
  • Demonstrating ensemble methods and threshold tuning
  • Understanding clinical risk factors in a dataset
  • Educational exploration of medical AI concepts
  • Portfolio and project demonstration purposes

This tool is NOT for

  • Making actual clinical decisions about real patients
  • Replacing physician judgment or clinical protocols
  • Determining discharge or admission policies
  • Any use in a live hospital or healthcare setting
  • Providing medical advice to individuals

Known Limitations

We are transparent about every limitation of this model. A responsible AI practitioner acknowledges what their system cannot do.

No socioeconomic data

Income, housing, social support, and lifestyle factors are strong predictors of readmission but are absent from this dataset.

Single dataset source

Trained on one publicly available dataset. Performance may differ significantly on data from different hospitals, countries, or populations.

Static model

Trained once on a fixed snapshot. It does not update with new patient outcomes or reflect changing clinical practices.

60% accuracy ceiling

Readmission is influenced by many factors outside hospital records. Perfect prediction from clinical data alone is not achievable.

Recall–specificity tradeoff

The threshold 0.42 increases recall to 75% but drops specificity to 47%. Nearly half of low-risk patients may be flagged unnecessarily.

Potential demographic bias

The training dataset reflects patterns from its source population. Performance across different ethnic, age, or socioeconomic groups has not been independently validated.

Design Principles

The following principles guided every decision in this project — from model selection to interface design.

1

Transparency over performance theatre

We report 60% accuracy honestly rather than selecting metrics that inflate the result. Every limitation on this page reflects a genuine constraint we identified and disclosed.

2

Clinical cost-awareness in model design

The choice to maximise recall over accuracy reflects real clinical logic — a false negative (missed high-risk patient) carries greater cost than a false positive. The model was tuned to reflect this asymmetry.

3

Human oversight is non-negotiable

Every prediction result page carries a visible disclaimer. The AI summary is explicitly framed as clinical decision support — not a diagnosis or treatment recommendation.

4

Privacy by design

No patient data entered into MedPredict is stored on any server. Prediction history is stored only in the user's local browser using localStorage and never transmitted.

5

Educational intent

This project exists to demonstrate that thoughtful ML engineering, clinical reasoning, and responsible AI thinking can coexist — even at a first-year student level.
